FE QUEST PWA v104
Distractor naturalness second-pass audit

Purpose
- Continue the v103 distractor-quality work.
- Remove another common test-writing clue: incorrect choices that openly announce their incompleteness through wording such as “do X and omit Y”, “leave Y unchanged”, “delay Y”, or similar patterns.
- Make learners reject distractors because the priority, scope, timing, mechanism, or target is wrong—not because the sentence sounds intentionally careless.

What changed
1. Subject A judgment questions
- Reworked affected distractors involving transactions, concurrent updates, deadlock, OSS licensing, communication management, and SLA improvement.
- Replaced intentionally careless choices with technically plausible but still inferior alternatives.

2. Subject B security case questions
- Reworked affected distractors in ransomware response, backup/recovery, suspicious-login response, risk treatment, SQL injection, XSS, password spraying, removable-media control, data exfiltration, password-hash leakage, signed software updates, and patch management.
- Incorrect choices now more often represent:
  * a reasonable action in the wrong order,
  * a mitigation that does not solve the root problem,
  * a narrower control than the question requires,
  * a related security mechanism aimed at another threat,
  * an over-broad or under-scoped response.

3. Correct-answer stylistic cue
- Shortened one Subject A network-management correct option that was materially longer than all three distractors.
- High-risk correct-answer length outliers are now 0 under the v104 heuristic.

Regression rules
- Retains the v103 check against “○○だけ…” and gratuitous absolute wording.
- Adds a second check for action-oriented distractors that reveal themselves through explicit omission/delay wording.

Design principle going forward
A distractor should look like something a learner with partial knowledge could genuinely choose. It should usually fail because of subject-matter reasoning, not because it contains a conspicuous phrase such as “only”, “do nothing”, “ignore”, or “leave unchanged”.
